# The Deck

`deck.html` — 47 slides assembled from the [overview](../README.md).
It opens in a browser, is navigated by scrolling, prints to PDF (`Ctrl+P`) and
works in both light and dark themes.

## What is inside

The first eight slides stand on their own and answer everything a reader needs:
a one-page summary, the cost of the problem in money, the product in a minute
with **real screens from live production**, what clients say, the effect for the
country, and who built it. The rest is the full breakdown — market, every module,
architecture, security, economics and horizons.

## Where the numbers come from

Every figure is reconciled against the code (`origin/main`) and the production
database on **24 August 2026**. The deck is a **snapshot**: unlike the overview
itself, it is not recalculated automatically.

**On the document counter.** Of the 137,617 documents the system serves,
**136,879 are a mirror of what businesses were already doing in Didox** and
**740 were created inside the product**. The whole history is pulled in so that
deals assemble across past years. The deck states this ratio itself, on the
summary slide and again on "Where we stand".

**On the deal counter.** Some rows in the cycle engine close as cleanup
artefacts — duplicates, empty shells, historical residue. As of 24 August 2026
that is 34,275 of 135,704. The deck shows **101,429** — chains excluding the
cleanup — and **75%** as the share completed in full.

## Product screens

The screenshots are taken from the live product. Counterparty names are masked;
the figures belong to the founder's own company and are shown with consent. The
interface runs in Russian and Uzbek — the two languages of the market it serves.
